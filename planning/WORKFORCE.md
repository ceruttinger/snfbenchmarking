# Workforce and policy extension

The 2017–2024 workforce sidecar queries three selected fields from official CMS JSON APIs: payroll employee FTEs, approved-program intern/resident FTEs, and Worksheet A total salaries. No raw distribution files are downloaded. The sidecar retains only derived selected observations and provenance. All 48,193 financial reports matched the workforce refresh by report ID, CCN and fiscal begin/end. Later API refreshes may contain revisions under the same report ID; this join does not prove the same revision vintage as frozen financial inputs. The app states that limitation.

Semantic catalog 1.1.0 adds seven measures: payroll FTEs, resident FTEs, annualized salaries, payroll FTEs per 100 reported beds, annualized discharges per FTE, annualized patient days per FTE, and annualized salaries per FTE. FTE period averages are never annualized. Zero denominators are excluded; nonfinite, blank or negative workforce inputs are unavailable. Missing residents are not assumed to mean no training program. Salary/FTE compares different worksheet scopes and is a screening measure requiring reconciliation, not occupational wages or total compensation.

The guided workforce view uses the existing target, geography, ownership, size and reporting-year controls, with fixed, balanced or annually reselected peers. Peer medians require five valid hospitals. Two workforce question examples are available to the AI planner; unsupported nurse ratios, vacancies, turnover and contract labor claims are explicitly refused.

National research summaries have their own visibly stated cohorts. Rural vulnerability shows annual general short-term/CAH groups; matched 2019/2024 capacity and workforce views require eligible, nonoverlapping periods. Matched capacity: 4,143 hospitals. Workforce: 4,115 hospitals with positive payroll FTEs and patient days at both endpoints; 1,972 increased patient days per FTE. Separate hospital medians are 4.48% payroll FTE growth and 2.65% annualized patient-day growth. These are descriptive, without case-mix, outpatient-work, inflation, merger or campus-boundary adjustment. They cannot establish shortage, closure or a policy/acquisition effect. Extreme changes need report review.

Rebuild in order: `python3 ai/build_workforce.py` (network); `python3 ai/build_semantics.py`; `python3 ai/build_policy.py`; `python3 graph/build_graph.py`; workforce/engine/graph tests; `python3 infra/package.py`. Deploy the app through existing `infra/deploy.py`, and import graph changes through `graph/update.py`. The graph update uses MERGE, preserves existing ownership and previous semantic catalog lineage, adds separate workforce source edges, and never retrieves the Neo4j password to the local machine. A temporary IAM policy grants only the new artifact's object key and is removed after the import. A previously stopped EC2 instance is restored to stopped state afterward.

`graph/workforce.cypher` provides six read-only workforce inspection queries. The original twenty policy queries are available through the research-query link. The deployed graph still has a bounded organization NPPES sample, not national individual clinician employment data. Broader occupation-specific sources remain future integrations; no unsupported affiliations are fabricated.
